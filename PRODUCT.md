# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Friends, peers, and Jeremy's own future self. Casual, low-stakes visits — someone checking in on what he's building or reading, not a recruiter or client evaluating credibility. No pressure to perform for outside judgment.

## Product Purpose

A lightweight personal hub: a short bio, a list of projects, and a curated reading list, plus contact links. Explicitly not a resume or portfolio play. Success looks like a friend clicking through to a project out of genuine interest — a soft nudge, not a conversion goal.

## Brand Personality

**Quiet craftsman.** Calm and considered, warm and personal — reads like a person, not a brand. Confidence without noise.

References:
- [una.im](https://una.im/) — the clean intro treatment and socials placed right up top, immediately orienting.
- [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski/) — the simplicity is close to right, but leans too spare; this site should carry a bit more warmth/texture than pure minimalism.

## Anti-references

Corporate resume/portfolio sites — anything that reads like it's optimized for recruiter credibility signals rather than a genuine personal page. Also avoid the generic SaaS-template look (gradient heroes, tracked-caps eyebrows, cream/sand default backgrounds).

## Design Principles

- **Calm over loud** — quiet confidence; no shouting for attention, no hype-driven layout tropes.
- **Warmth without gloss** — approachable and human, but with real craft and texture, not corporate polish.
- **Orient immediately** — take the cue from una.im: intro and contact/socials surfaced early, no scroll-hunting to find out who this is.
- **Simplicity with texture** — closer to emilkowal.ski's restraint than to a busy portfolio, but with enough personality (color, type, motion) that it doesn't feel bare.
- **No performance for strangers** — every design decision should serve friends/peers/future-self, not an imagined recruiter audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support for any animation. No additional requirements beyond solid baseline practice.
